## 1. Pronoun — Definition

A **pronoun** is a word used in place of a noun to avoid repetition.

**Example:** Ram went to the market and Ram found a bag. → Ram went to the market and **he** found a bag.

---

## 2. Personal Pronouns

Personal pronouns are related to **person**.

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st person | I | we |
| 2nd person | you | you |
| 3rd person | he, she, it | they |

### Pronoun Case Table

| Person | Subjective / Nominative | Objective | Possessive Adjective | Possessive Pronoun |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | I / we | me / us | my / our | mine / ours |
| 2nd | you | you | your | yours |
| 3rd | he / she / it / they | him / her / it / them | his / her / its / their | his / hers / — / theirs |

---

## 3. Subjective vs Objective Case

### Formula
**Subjective/Nominative + Verb + Objective**

Examples from the class:
- **She met him.**
- **They invited me.**

### Remember
- Subject → **I, he, she, we, they**
- Object → **me, him, her, us, them**

---

## 4. Pronoun After Preposition

### Golden Rule
**Preposition + Objective Case**

Examples:
- between **you and me**
- except **him**
- for **me**
- to **me**

### SSC Trap
❌ Between you and I  
✅ **Between you and me**

❌ Everyone except he  
✅ **Everyone except him**

---

## 5. Possessive Adjective vs Possessive Pronoun

### Possessive Adjective + Noun
- This is **my car**.
- This is **his book**.
- This is **its cover**.

### Possessive Pronoun — No Noun
- This car is **mine**.
- This book is **his**.
- This house is **ours**.

### Quick Rule
**my / his / our / their + noun**

**mine / his / ours / theirs → noun omitted**

The class slide specifically notes that **its** does not have a separate possessive-pronoun form in this pattern.

---

## 6. Possessive Adjective + V4 (-ing)

The class emphasizes:

### **Possessive Adjective + V4 (-ing)**

Examples:
- I was confident of **my winning** the race.
- She was not confident of **her doing** well in the examination.
- She is sure of **my helping** others.

### Memory Pattern
**Preposition + Possessive Adjective + V4**

---

## 7. Pronoun Sequence / Order

The class teaches the preferred sequence:

# **2 + 3 + 1**

**2nd person + 3rd person + 1st person**

### Quick Memory
**You → He/She/They → I/We**

Example:
- **You, he and I** are going to play cricket.

The slides contrast this preferred “good sense” order with an undesirable order.

---

## 8. Compound Subject + Agreement

Examples shown in the class:
- **You and they are responsible.**
- **You and he made a mistake.**
- **You and he will win the match.**
- **You and they have done a great job.**

For a group containing **You + he/she + I**, the class uses **our** when the possessive refers to the whole group.

Example:
- You, he and I have done **our** job.

---

## 9. Comparison with Pronouns

### Subject–Subject
Use the subject form when the pronoun represents the subject of an implied clause.

- **He is taller than I.** (= than I am)

### Object–Object
Use the object form when the pronoun functions as an object.

- He knows **them** better than **me**.

### Possessive Comparison
When the noun is omitted, use the possessive pronoun.

- My house is bigger than **yours**. (= your house)
- Your house is bigger than **mine**. (= my house)

### Compare Like with Like
The class emphasizes:
- Subject ↔ Subject
- Object ↔ Object
- City ↔ City
- Language ↔ Language
- State ↔ State

# ⚡ Ultra-Quick SSC Revision

1. **Subject →** I, he, she, we, they
2. **Object →** me, him, her, us, them
3. **After preposition →** objective case
4. **my/his/our/their →** normally + noun
5. **mine/his/ours/theirs →** noun omitted
6. **Possessive adjective + V4 →** my winning, her doing
7. **Preferred sequence → 2 + 3 + 1**
8. **Compound subject →** I, not me
9. **Compound object →** me/him/her/us/them
10. **Comparison →** compare like with like and use the appropriate case
