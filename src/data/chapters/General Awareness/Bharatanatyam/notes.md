# Bharatanatyam

Bharatanatyam is one of the oldest and most widely practised classical dance forms of India, tracing its roots to the temples of Tamil Nadu.

## Origins

- Traditionally performed as a temple art (*Sadir*) by *Devadasis*.
- Codified in the ancient Sanskrit text **Natya Shastra**, attributed to sage Bharata Muni — one theory behind the name "Bharata-natyam".
- Revived in its modern stage form in the early 20th century, notably through the efforts of Rukmini Devi Arundale.

## Core Elements

Bharatanatyam performances are built around three core aspects:

1. **Nritta** — pure rhythmic dance movement, without emotional expression.
2. **Nritya** — expressive dance that conveys meaning through *mudras* (hand gestures) and facial expression.
3. **Natya** — the dramatic, storytelling aspect combining dance with drama.

## Repertoire (Margam)

A traditional recital typically follows a sequence known as the *margam*:

| Item | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Alarippu | Invocatory, pure dance piece |
| Jatiswaram | Rhythmic dance set to musical notes |
| Shabdam | Introduces expressive storytelling |
| Varnam | The centrepiece — combines nritta and nritya |
| Padam | Slow, expressive piece |
| Tillana | Fast-paced, closing item |
